## Task: weekly China energy market update

Write a weekly update on China's nuclear and renewable energy market as a whole — not the investor's watchlist. They want to understand where the industry is heading: what Beijing decided, what got built or approved, where prices and margins are going, and what that means for who wins. The bundle has this week's Chinese headlines grouped by theme (nuclear, solar, wind, storage, grid, policy, thorium) and last week's update for continuity.

Do your own research too — the headlines are a starting point, not the whole picture. Search for this week's official data and announcements: National Energy Administration (国家能源局) monthly installation and generation figures, State Council reactor approvals, NDRC pricing policy, polysilicon/module/lithium carbonate prices, big tenders and grid projects, and English coverage (Reuters, Bloomberg, Caixin, SCMP, Carbon Brief, Ember) where it adds context. Prefer primary sources and cite them as links.

Structure (bold labels, this order; skip a section if there's genuinely nothing):

**The big picture** — two or three sentences: the most important development this week and the direction of travel.

**Nuclear** — approvals, construction milestones, grid connections, fuel cycle, Gen-IV (thorium/TMSR, HTGR, fast reactors, SMRs), safety regulation.

**Solar** — installations, polysilicon/wafer/module prices, the "anti-involution" capacity and price discipline, exports and trade measures.

**Wind** — installations and approvals, offshore, turbine prices, exports.

**Storage & grid** — storage installations and tenders, cell prices, UHV lines, grid investment, market reforms (spot markets, renewable pricing).

**Policy & demand** — power demand, coal vs clean generation, curtailment, anything from the NEA/NDRC/State Council.

**What it means for the watchlist** — one or two lines at most, only where a development clearly bears on a name in the table.

Bullets within sections, each one specific and quantified where possible ("NEA: 212 GW solar added Jan–Sep, +18% y/y"). Ignore stock-price chatter and non-China stories. Keep it under 550 words.
