## Task: weekly brief

Write the investor's weekly brief on their watchlist. This is the main thing they read, so it has to earn its place: a smart friend who follows Chinese energy stocks, telling them what changed this week and what deserves their attention. The bundle has the full watchlist with returns vs index, distance from their buy limits, this week's classified filings, Chinese headlines per company, theme news (e.g. thorium/TMSR), and last week's brief.

Structure (use these bold labels, in this order; skip a section only if it would be empty):

**The week in one line** — the single most important thing.

**Market** — what drove Chinese/HK equities this week and whether the watchlist moved with the market or diverged. Two or three sentences. The bundle rarely explains the market itself, so always search for it first (e.g. "China stocks week" plus the dates, or "A股 本周 下跌 原因") and name the actual driver — sector rotation, policy, rates, flows.

**What changed** — bullets, one per company that had real news (filings, results, contracts, buybacks, insider moves, litigation, policy). Translate and quantify. Say whether each strengthens or weakens the thesis. Leave out companies with nothing new.

**Entry radar** — the investor buys dips on intact theses. Using the table: which names are at or below their limit, and for each, is the weakness market/sector-driven (3m vs index near zero or positive) or stock-specific (well below index)? Name the one or two that look most like the setup they want, and the ones where the weakness has a company-specific reason that needs understanding first. If limits look stale — set when prices were far higher and now far above the market — say so and suggest how they might reset them (e.g. relative to the 52-week low, or staged), without pretending to certainty.

**Thesis check** — one or two observations where the evidence this week bears on a thesis claim: the thorium supply-chain names (Hailu, Zhefu) and TMSR milestones, the renewables/storage build-out names, rare earths. Include theme news that matters. Be candid if a thesis is looking weaker.

**Next week** — known upcoming events (results dates, policy meetings, data releases) if you can find them.

Keep it under 500 words — cut the least important points rather than compressing everything. A scoreboard table is appended automatically — don't recreate it.
